# Literature Update and Novelty Audit

**Project:** Risk-Aware Explainable Phishing URL Detection Using Confidence and Explanation Reliability  
**Search window requested:** 7 October–18 November 2026 (inclusive)  
**Prepared:** 18 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Supplement the existing literature review and matrix without changing them. This is a targeted update, not a systematic or exhaustive review.

## Scope and evidence rules

The existing literature matrix and Phase 2 review were inspected before this update. This search prioritizes publisher pages and accessible full papers. A date inside the requested window can mean online publication, indexing in a publisher database, or a repository posting; where these differ, the distinction is stated. A publisher page confirms what the paper reports, not independent replication of its results.

Evidence labels used below:

- **Full text checked:** publisher full text or PDF was available and inspected for the stated facts.
- **Publisher record/abstract:** title, authors, status and abstract-level claims verified on the publisher record, but the full text was not available or not fully inspected.
- **Preprint record:** repository record or abstract; not peer-reviewed evidence unless separately established.
- **Proposal only:** the article itself describes a proposed method and reports no experiments.
- **Unverified:** a detail could not be established from the accessible primary record and is not presented as fact.

## Findings that materially affect the novelty audit

1. **URL-level explanation stability in phishing detection is already studied.** Ahamed et al. published an integrated robustness/evaluation protocol on 31 August 2026, before this update window. On PHIUSIIL plus a PhishTank phishing set, they evaluate explanation stability using SHAP top-K Jaccard similarity under structured URL perturbations. They report a fall to 0.526–0.535 under subdomain injection, alongside perturbation-induced predictive failures. Their contribution is a robustness evaluation protocol, not an abstention rule or confidence-versus-stability selective-risk comparison. This rules out claiming that URL phishing explanation stability itself is new. [Publisher full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1834407/full)

2. **A November paper jointly covers calibration, explanation stability, and constrained perturbations.** Patel et al. (published 1 November 2026 on the journal page) report domain-grouped PhiUSIIL evaluation, calibration/Brier score, TreeSHAP explanation behavior, and constrained feature perturbation. Its abstract reports strong clean metrics but also substantial attack success and attribution change. The accessible publisher landing page does not establish that explanation stability is used to select or defer predictions, nor does its abstract report a matched-coverage comparison against a confidence-only selective policy. The full PDF could not be inspected through the available publisher link; treat details beyond its landing-page record/abstract as unverified. [Publisher article record](https://ijaicet.com/index.php/ijaicet/article/view/41)

3. **“Stability + uncertainty” is not wholly unproposed.** An IJREK article dated 28 October 2026 proposes feature-selection stability across resamples/time alongside uncertainty-based abstention for phishing URL detection. The paper explicitly frames these as proposed design choices; it does not establish completed implementation or experimentally measured benefits. Its feature stability is global feature-selection/ranking consistency, not local per-instance explanation stability. It therefore narrows broad conceptual novelty claims but does not answer the project’s specific empirical question. Peer-review status was not established from the accessible article page. [Article record](https://www.ijrek.org/article/IJREK-2026-00157)

4. **Calibrated confidence-based routing is also present in a close phishing website study.** PhishLens (21 October 2026) uses a calibrated probability/uncertainty band to route ambiguous cases to a local LLM for verified evidence selection. The classifier’s label and probability remain frozen; the routing primarily selects an explanation/evidence pathway rather than withholding the classifier decision. It does not test attribution stability or compare confidence-only against confidence-plus-stability at matched coverage. [Publisher article](https://ijemr.vandanapublications.com/index.php/j/article/view/1951) · [Full paper](https://ijemr.vandanapublications.com/index.php/j/article/download/1951/2070/4111)

5. **No directly matching study was verified in this update.** In the records inspected, no study tested the exact incremental value of a local explanation-stability score as a selective decision signal over a properly calibrated confidence-only policy at matched coverage on both in-domain and shifted phishing URL data. This is a bounded search finding, not proof that no such study exists.

## Synthesis by topic

### Phishing URL selective prediction and uncertainty-based deferral

The project’s baseline concept is not new. Ghalechyan et al. discuss interval-based unknown predictions; OACSP-Forensics is a preprint on calibrated selective classification/coverage-risk that includes phishing URLs; PhishLens routes uncertain cases for downstream evidence selection; and the October IJREK article proposes uncertainty-based abstention. These differ materially in unit of deferral, publication status, model inputs, and whether results exist. In particular, routing an explanation step while preserving the classifier label is not equivalent to abstaining from an automatic phishing/benign decision.

### Calibration under distribution shift

OACSP is the closest located work on selective prediction under phishing-related shift, but its full paper was not available to verify. MAG-Phish X highlights that calibration/threshold policies must match the feature-availability condition. PhishLens reports an external dataset, but its paper also discloses an earlier project-version exposure. The new temporal-split study is highly limited and confounds temporal and group/composition changes. Robust conclusions require independent, time/domain-grouped evaluation and calibration-fit data separated from policy tuning and final test.

### Explanation stability

Explanation stability under URL perturbation has direct prior art: Ahamed et al. measure SHAP top-K Jaccard under syntactically valid mutations. The November IJAICET abstract also reports attribution changes under feature perturbation. These works treat stability as an evaluation/robustness outcome. The located records do not establish that local explanation stability itself is used as a per-instance deferral signal and tested for incremental selective-risk benefit over calibrated confidence at matched coverage.

### Adjacent negative/skeptical evidence

The HIEF phishing-email study by Del-Valle-Soto et al. (*Algorithms*, 19(9), 741; published 1 September 2026; DOI 10.3390/a19090741) is outside the update window. Its publisher abstract verifies calibrated evidence fusion, group-aware splits, source-held-out testing and a cross-source specificity collapse to 0.041. The existing project review notes a selective-risk comparison and AURC values of .044 for calibrated probability and .084 for evidential uncertainty; those exact values were **not independently located in the accessible publisher abstract during this refresh**, so treat them as inherited/unreverified until the article body or supplement is checked. If confirmed, this is useful negative adjacent evidence: a richer uncertainty signal need not rank errors better than calibrated confidence. The study is email rather than URL phishing and does not test explanation stability. [Publisher article](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4893/19/9/741)

## Novelty boundaries after refresh

### No longer defensible

- “First phishing URL detector to use explanations/SHAP/LIME.”
- “First phishing URL study of explanation stability.”
- “First phishing URL study to evaluate uncertainty, abstention, or selective prediction.”
- “First study to combine calibration, explanation stability and perturbation analysis.” The November IJAICET abstract already covers those broad ingredients.
- “No one proposed feature stability plus uncertainty abstention for phishing URLs.” The October IJREK paper proposes this combination, though feature-level and without reported experiments.

### Narrow contribution still plausibly defensible, subject to a reproducible final search

The most defensible claim is an **incremental empirical test**: whether a per-instance local explanation-stability signal computed under a pre-specified, semantically valid URL-perturbation protocol adds useful selection information beyond a properly calibrated confidence-only policy, evaluated at matched coverage on in-domain and shifted data. Do not claim this combination is absolutely unprecedented. Identify Ahamed et al. and the November IJAICET article as adjacent work and explain the distinction: they report stability/robustness, but the verified records do not use stability to improve selective-risk decisions at matched coverage.

Novelty depends on research design quality: perturbations must preserve the URL’s intended class/meaning by a defensible validation procedure; stability must be computed without test-label leakage; confidence must be calibrated on separate data; and B/C coverage must be matched on each evaluation domain or shift condition. Otherwise, apparent gains can arise from policy tuning or distribution leakage rather than explanation stability.

## Recommendation

The current research question and A/B/C comparison remain **provisionally defensible**, but only as a narrow, carefully qualified incremental-value question. The Ahamed and IJAICET studies prevent a general novelty claim about perturbation-based explanation stability; OACSP, Ghalechyan and other uncertainty-routing work prevent a general novelty claim about confidence-based deferral. The project’s potential contribution is the controlled, matched-coverage test of whether local explanation stability adds value beyond calibrated confidence under in-domain and shifted evaluation. Retain the existing exact question pending the user/mentor’s decision; this review does not change it.
